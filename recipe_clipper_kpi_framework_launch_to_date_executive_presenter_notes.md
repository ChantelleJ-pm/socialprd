# Recipe Clipper launch-to-date KPI slide - speaker notes

These notes are for the executive KPI slide:

- `Recipe_Clipper_KPI_Framework_Launch_to_Date_Executive_Slide.png`

They are written to sound like a PM talking through the slide, not reading a script.

---

## Main message

The short version of this slide is that Recipe Clipper is showing real launch-to-date value, and the save experience is strong enough that it is worth continuing to invest in.

This is meant to be a high-level slide, so I would use it to anchor the conversation before going deeper into friction or UX follow-ups.

---

## Suggested talk track

What I wanted this slide to do is simplify the KPI story down to the biggest launch-to-date numbers.

At the top, there are three headline metrics I would anchor on.

First, we have **8,624 successful recipes saved** since launch. That is the clearest measure of product value actually delivered so far.

Second, we are at a **51.1% save success rate** launch-to-date. So across the full launch period, about half of save attempts are ending successfully.

Third, average **save-click time is 4.65 seconds**. So once users try to save, the flow is relatively fast.

Then underneath that, I included three supporting diagnostics that help explain the overall picture.

We have **23,803 Add Recipe clicks**, which tells us there is real adoption and demand for the feature.

We have a **25.9% user error rate**, which is still meaningful and tells us there is still friction on the input side.

And we have **81.7% external-brand share**, which is important because it confirms people are using Recipe Clipper for its intended job: saving recipes from outside MyRecipes, not just re-saving internal content.

So the way I would summarize this slide is: Recipe Clipper has already delivered meaningful value at launch scale, and the remaining opportunities are mostly about improving conversion and reducing avoidable friction.

## One-minute version

This is the simple launch-to-date view. Since launch, Recipe Clipper has driven **8,624 successful saves**, is converting at **51.1%**, and the average save-click time is **4.65 seconds**. We also know there is real demand, with **23,803 Add Recipe clicks**, and that the feature is being used for the intended external-use case, with **81.7% external-brand share**. The biggest remaining issue visible in this view is user-side friction, reflected in the **25.9% user error rate**.

---

## How to explain each metric

### Successful recipes saved - 8,624
This is the cleanest value metric on the slide.

Why it matters:
- it represents actual value created, not just interest
- it is better than clicks alone because it reflects completed outcomes

### Save success rate - 51.1%
This is the high-level health metric for the save flow.

Why it matters:
- it tells us how often save attempts end successfully
- it is the clearest operational signal of flow quality

### Avg save-click time - 4.65s
This is the speed metric.

Why it matters:
- speed affects perceived quality
- faster save completion usually supports trust and repeat use

### Add Recipe clicks - 23,803
This is the top-of-funnel demand signal.

Why it matters:
- it tells us people are discovering and trying the feature
- it helps separate adoption issues from conversion issues

### User error rate - 25.9%
This is the clearest friction metric on the slide.

Why it matters:
- it shows a meaningful amount of failure is still happening before users get value
- it points to UX and guidance opportunities, not just backend fixes

### External-brand share - 81.7%
This is the product-fit / use-case validation metric.

Why it matters:
- it shows the feature is being used for external clipping, which is the actual product promise

---

## What not to overclaim from this slide

I would avoid saying this proves:
- retention
- repeat usage
- collection creation
- long-term habit formation

Those are still important, but this export does not give us the user-level metrics we would need to make that case confidently.

---

## If someone asks why the north-star is not on the slide

You can say:

> The KPI I would ideally use as the north-star is **successful saves per active user**, because it combines adoption and delivered value. We do not have that user-based metric in this export, so this slide uses the strongest launch-to-date proxies we do have.

---

## If someone asks why external-brand share is included

You can say:

> I included external-brand share because it tells us whether the product is being used for the intended behavior. If that number were low, it would suggest the feature was not really fulfilling its broader role. At **81.7%**, it tells us the product is doing the job it was built to do.

---

## If someone asks what the biggest opportunity is from this slide

You can say:

> The biggest opportunity visible here is reducing user-side friction. The demand is there and the product is generating successful saves, but the user error rate shows we still have room to improve the input experience and overall completion rate.
