# Recipe Clipper Stakeholder Slides

## Slide 1 - Recipe Clipper performance improved in stages

### Slide title
Recipe Clipper performance improved in stages

### Slide subtitle
A three-phase view shows improvement through the transition window and into the cleaner post-mid-Feb period.

### On-slide callouts

- **Average daily save attempt success rate**
  - **40.5% -> 47.2% -> 52.1%**
- **Average save-click time**
  - **5.1s -> 4.7s -> 3.8s**
- **External-brand share of successful saves**
  - **79.0% -> 79.1% -> 85.3%**

### Supporting bullets

- **126 supported sites** were added on **Feb 9** during the transition period
- Performance improves in stages, with gains visible in the transition window and further gains in the trusted post-mid-Feb period
- Collections and repeat usage remain important KPIs, but they are not measurable in this export

### Speaker notes

- Use the **three-phase progression** for the topline story:
  - **12/18-1/30** baseline
  - **1/31-2/18** transition period
  - **2/19-3/18** trusted post-mid-Feb period
- Use **2/19 onward** as the cleanest read on error attribution because the save URL error categories were updated around mid-Feb.
- Frame this as progress on the leading KPIs visible in the export: save attempt success rate, save-click time, and external recipe adoption.

## Slide 2 - Post-mid-Feb error mix shows site coverage is the main remaining scaling constraint

### Slide title
Post-mid-Feb error mix shows site coverage is the main remaining scaling constraint

### Slide subtitle
From **2/19 onward**, error attribution is the cleanest read of user vs product vs external friction.

### On-slide callouts

- **Outcome mix of save attempts, post-mid-Feb**
  - **52.1%** success
  - **24.5%** user-side errors
  - **12.6%** external/domain errors
  - **10.0%** product-side errors

- **92.1% of external errors are unsupported-site related**
  - The planned **300+ additional sites** is the right next lever

- **84.2% of product errors come from general_fail + bookmark_fail**
  - Product cleanup can stay focused and targeted

### Supporting bullets

- Do **not** headline product issues as "20%"; that figure is the share of failures, not the share of attempts
- The clearer stakeholder message is that the core product is working for a majority of attempts, while the biggest remaining scaling constraint is site coverage
- The next best moves are:
  - add the next 300+ supported sites
  - keep reducing user input friction
  - continue targeted cleanup of general and bookmark failures

### Speaker notes

- If asked about the "21%" number, clarify that product issues are about **10% of save attempts** and about **21% of classified failures**.
- This keeps the conversation accurate and prevents stakeholders from inferring that the core product is broadly broken.
- The strategic takeaway is that domain expansion is now a data-backed growth and reliability lever, not just backlog maintenance.
